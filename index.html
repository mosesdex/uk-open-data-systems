Groundtruth

British government publishes records that say what happened but not, in any form a computer can match, where it happened or who it involved. Groundtruth resolves those two things — and thirteen systems become possible that are not possible today.

The problem

Two missing joins

The Environment Agency publishes every planning application it objected to on flood risk grounds — 23,336 records — with no address, no postcode and no coordinate. It cannot map its own objections, and for 7,011 of them it does not know what the council decided.

The Ministry of Housing runs a national schema holding £1,491,818,575 of developer obligations. Not one record carries a location. The property identifier field in the linked planning dataset is populated in 0.0% of records; the address field, in the same records, is populated in 98.1%.

Councils receive school capacity data for 3,651 pupil planning areas whose boundaries are published nowhere. The Environment Agency records the owner of its flood defences as unknown on 73.7% of 141,629 assets.

On the other side: procurement supplier records resolve inconsistently to companies, care providers are published by name with no company number, grid registers share no key between transmission and distribution, and the register of UK public sector organisations was decommissioned — its domains no longer resolve at all.

Every one of those is the same two problems. Resolving a place. Resolving an organisation. Groundtruth builds both as open infrastructure, and the systems above stop being impossible.

Compounding

How they work together

Two resolution layers are what make thirteen systems one platform rather than thirteen products. But the reason it is worth building as a platform is not the shared plumbing — it is that a single event or a single reference reaches across several systems at once.

Each chain below starts with one fact arriving. Nothing in any of them requires new data; every step runs on sources that return data to an anonymous request.

ENTITY SPINE

A company enters insolvency proceedings

1
Watchman
Reads the notice from the official insolvency record, which carries a structured company number — so the organisation is identified without name matching.
2
Bellwether
Checks that company against the care registers. If it operates care homes, children’s homes or special schools, the councils depending on it are known immediately — including any where it holds a large share of local capacity.
3
Sentinel
Checks the same company against the public procurement record. Which contracts does it hold, with which buyers, worth how much.
4
Ledger
If it is a developer, checks what contributions it owes and whether they have been paid.
What that gives you
An exposure answer in minutes, for an event the public sector currently discovers by reading the news. This is the capability whose absence was the lesson of the 2018 contractor collapse.
PLACE SPINE

A planning permission is granted

1
Plumbline
Records the decision and how long it actually took — against the statutory deadline, not the extended one.
2
Ledger
Attaches the developer contributions agreed with it, and tracks whether they are received and spent.
3
Sightline
Checks whether a statutory consultee objected, and whether the objection was followed.
4
Highwater
Checks whether the site sits in a flood zone, and whether the Environment Agency advised against it.
5
Catchment
Feeds the homes into school place forecasting for the planning area it falls in.
6
Lastmile
Checks whether the site has gigabit infrastructure, and whether the homes were built connectable.
What that gives you
Six systems reading one planning reference. Today each of those questions is answered separately, by different bodies, at different geographies, mostly not at all.
BOTH SPINES

An asset has no recorded owner

1
Bulwark
Holds 141,629 flood defences, 73.7% with owner recorded as unknown, all geolocated.
2
Place spine
Resolves each asset to the land parcel it sits on.
3
Entity spine
Resolves that parcel to an organisation, where one is registered.
4
Watchman
Flags where that organisation is in financial distress — because a defence owned by a failing company is a different risk from one owned by a solvent one.
What that gives you
Closing a gap the Environment Agency reports on three-quarters of its own asset register, using only the resolution capability the platform already has.
ENTITY SPINE

A regulator changes its identifier scheme

1
Baseline
Storm overflow identifiers changed in 2024 — the published data still carries the old identifier in a separate field, and multi-year analysis breaks across the change.
2
Junction
Grid connection registers share no identifier between transmission and distribution, so the same project appears twice with no link.
3
Entity spine
The same resolution technique applied to physical assets rather than organisations, with match confidence published rather than asserted.
4
Bellwether
The same problem again in care — owner names published without company numbers.
What that gives you
One capability, four sectors. Identity breaks are the single most common reason a national dataset cannot be analysed over time, and almost nobody fixes them.

And the work compounds backwards

The compounding also runs the other way. Work done for one system lowers the cost of the next.

01
The planning reference resolver
Built for Ledger, reused by Highwater, Sightline, Plumbline and Catchment. Five systems, one hard problem solved once.
02
The corporate graph
Assembled for Sentinel, reused by Bellwether, Watchman and Freehold. The beneficial ownership register is a free daily download, so the marginal cost of the fourth consumer is near zero.
03
Coverage reporting
Every system publishes what share of England it actually represents. Built once as a discipline, applied everywhere — and it is the thing that makes the outputs trustworthy to a statistician.
04
Match confidence
No system asserts a resolution it cannot evidence. The same confidence machinery serves all thirteen, and it is what makes the work defensible when a named organisation objects.
The whole point

One problem, thirteen times

Read the twenty-six examples below and the same sentence appears in every one: the record exists, but it cannot be joined to anything.

The Environment Agency knows it objected to an application. It does not know where. A ministry knows a developer owes £1.49bn. It cannot say for which sites. A council knows a company runs its care homes. It cannot see the other eleven councils that depend on the same company. A regulator knows spills fell 35%. It cannot say how much of that was rain.

None of these is a missing dataset. Every one is a missing join — and there are only two of them. Resolve a place. Resolve an organisation.

That is why thirteen systems are one platform rather than thirteen products. The hard part is not the flood analysis, the procurement analysis or the care analysis; each of those is straightforward once the data lines up. The hard part is making the data line up, and it is the same hard part every time.

Build the two resolutions once as open infrastructure and thirteen questions that cannot currently be answered become answerable — then they compound, because a single event reaches several systems at once and each new system costs less than the last.

Twenty-six worked examples sit on the individual system pages — two apiece, each a situation somebody in government cannot currently resolve.

Sequence

Build order

The first move is unpaid on purpose. A company with no filed accounts does not win a departmental contract — it wins one after it has visibly fixed something.

1
Publish the planning area geography
Months 1–3
Open, unpaid, under an open licence. It is weeks of work, demonstrably missing, and it converts an unknown supplier into the people who fixed something government wanted fixed. The named team that owns the survey is the buyer for everything after.
2
Ship Catchment on top of it
Months 3–9
Small-area demand forecasting and the surplus-shortage adjacency map. The National Audit Office has already written the thesis: national projections are accurate, the failure is spatial.
3
Build the entity spine and Sentinel
Months 6–15
Organisation resolution first, then process indicators and framework concentration. Route findings through the compliance service, which is open to anyone, rather than the review service, which is not.
4
Extend the place spine to Highwater and Plumbline
Months 12–24
Both consume the same asset. Two further buyers in two further departments, at marginal cost.
5
Junction last
Months 18–30
It needs both spines mature, and four of six network operators must first be persuaded to serve data to anonymous clients.
Delivery

How government would actually buy this

Central digital spend controls ended on 1 April 2026. Departments are now accountable for applying the functional standards themselves, and assurance applies only above their own delegated authority limits. A pilot is a departmental decision rather than a central one.

Framework entry reopens. The current digital outcomes framework was let as an open framework under the Procurement Act, with reopening cycles — so missing a window is no longer fatal. Product-shaped systems route through the cloud framework; delivery-shaped ones through digital outcomes; unproven research through the small business research initiative, where the department funds development and the supplier retains the intellectual property.

Small supplier status is a scored advantage attached to a published target. Departments must set and publish three-year small business spend targets and report progress annually. They are collectively behind, and central government's small business share has fallen rather than risen.

What makes a proposition land

The counter-fraud programme is the template worth copying. Government published a loss estimate it owns, reported savings against it, credited data-matching explicitly, and translated the result into nurses and repaired roads. Every system here is framed the same way: against a published number the department already accepts, with recovered or avoided cost rather than capability uplift as the headline.

Two further things now count as compliance arguments rather than sales points. Open standards and data portability are required by the technology code of practice, and the central digital team for local government has named supplier lock-in and inconsistent interfaces as the problem it exists to solve. And there is a durable advantage in depending on nothing: every source these thirteen systems use is fetchable without an account, so there is no registration to revoke and no licence to terminate.

Why each is out

Transit and Threshold handle live social care records and placements for homeless children. They need a UK-resident, security-cleared operations tier. Year two, not never — and the largest money in the portfolio sits behind them. Freehold depends on ownership data that redirects to a sign-in under a licence with audit rights and a 48-hour delete clause. Waypoint and Hearth were killed by the research itself: government had already shipped a free replacement for one, and the other targets a market that is legally blocked and commercially served.

Method

Fifteen published claims were corrected.

Research overturned our own findings repeatedly — a premise that was already solved, a market that was legally blocked, a linkage key that does not exist in the published data, a trend that vanished once the rate behind it was computed, and one figure derived and then presented as though a document had said it. Roughly half of what sounded solid needed correction once someone checked the primary source. Every system brief carries its sources for that reason.
