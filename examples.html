01 · Children & families · Local government · Operations

Catchment

School place planning at the geography that matters

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A council with falling rolls closes a primary school. Two miles away another is over capacity and turning children away. Both facts are true, and neither is visible in council-level statistics.

What Catchment does

Catchment publishes the missing boundaries for all 3,651 areas, so empty seats and overcrowding show up side by side inside the same council.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

Basic Need capital is allocated at planning area and year group with no offsetting between areas — so £1.096bn is distributed on a geography that is published nowhere and cannot be mapped.

What Catchment does

Catchment rebuilds those boundaries from a list the department already publishes, and gives them away free.

02 · Fraud & integrity · Central government · Public money

Sentinel

Public procurement integrity and collusion detection

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

Three companies bid for a £4m council contract. Two of them share a director. Nobody notices, because neither record carries a company number and the buyer has no way to check.

What Sentinel does

Sentinel works out which real companies the three bidders are, sees that two of them share a director, and flags the bid as connected — before the contract is awarded, not after an investigation.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A framework worth £200m produces 400 call-offs across dozens of authorities. Six suppliers win most of them. No single buyer sees the pattern, because each call-off is one record in one authority.

What Sentinel does

Sentinel adds up every order placed under that framework across every authority, and shows that six firms are taking most of it. This is the clearest warning sign available in UK data.

03 · Climate & energy · Housing · Central government

Highwater

Floodplain development monitoring

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

The Environment Agency objects to 47 homes on flood risk grounds. The council approves them anyway. The Agency never learns the outcome — one of 7,011 cases where it does not know what was decided.

What Highwater does

Highwater finds the decision on the council’s own public planning website and fills the gap — giving a true national figure instead of the partial sample the Agency relies on now.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A journalist asks how many homes were built in flood zones against expert advice. Nobody can answer, because the objections file carries no address, postcode or coordinate.

What Highwater does

Highwater works out where each objection was, puts it on the flood map, and answers the question. Right now an insurance company supplies that answer to Parliament, using data it had to buy.

04 · Housing · Central government · Operations

Plumbline

Planning pipeline truth and delivery measurement

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A council reports 91% of major applications decided on time and is judged compliant. Measured against the actual statutory deadline it is 19%, because more than three-quarters used an agreed extension.

What Plumbline does

Plumbline always shows both numbers side by side, and shows how often extensions are being used to make the headline look better.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

The Housing Delivery Test that triggers presumption-in-favour sanctions was last measured on data ending March 2023. Councils and the department are both flying blind on the mechanism meant to deliver 1.5 million homes.

What Plumbline does

Plumbline keeps a running estimate going between official measurements, so everyone can see where housing delivery is heading instead of finding out two years later.

05 · Climate & energy · Operations · Central government

Junction

Unified grid connection queue intelligence

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A developer asks where they can connect 5MW within two years. Two network operators both report "available capacity" — computed on incompatible bases, because the specification delegates the calculation to each of them.

What Junction does

Junction converts everyone’s figures to one clear definition, and publishes each operator’s own method next to it — so the numbers can finally be compared.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

The same project appears in both the transmission and distribution registers under different identifiers, with no shared key. Counting either register alone is wrong; counting both double-counts.

What Junction does

Junction matches the two records to the same project and says how confident it is, rather than quietly guessing.

06 · Housing · Local government · Public money

Ledger

Developer contributions, made mappable

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A community was promised a school alongside a 400-home development. Six years on there is no school, and nobody can show whether the money was collected, spent, or is still sitting.

What Ledger does

Ledger ties the money to the site it was promised for, and shows whether it was ever collected and whether it was ever spent.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A council holds around £19m of unspent developer contributions and is asked publicly why. It cannot produce a spatial account of what was promised, for what, and where.

What Ledger does

Ledger puts every promise on a map — by council, by ward, by site, by what it was for. £1.49bn becomes visible for the first time.

07 · Children & families · Local government · Public money

Bellwether

Provider concentration and failure exposure

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A care operator running more than two-thirds of one borough’s care home beds gets into difficulty. The council finds out when it reaches the news.

What Bellwether does

Bellwether tracks that dependency all the time. The regulator’s own public list already names the company behind 93.9% of care home beds — nobody adds it up.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A children’s home group appears in the register under three slightly different owner names, so its true size is invisible — and statute now requires geographical concentration to be assessed.

What Bellwether does

Bellwether recognises the three names as one company. The same fix takes the biggest special school group from an apparent 30 schools to its real 51.

08 · Housing · Central government · Operations

Sightline

Statutory consultee advice, and what happened to it

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

Active Travel England advised on planning applications covering 521,000 homes in a single year and published none of it. Government is now cutting consultee involvement by around 40%, with no data on what that advice achieved.

What Sightline does

Sightline records what was advised, what was decided and what got built — so the change can be judged on evidence rather than assumption.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A council overrides expert advice on a development. Residents want to know whether that is unusual. No published source can tell them.

What Sightline does

Sightline publishes how often each council overrules expert advice, with the context — including that the rate has actually been flat for nine years, even though the raw numbers rose.

09 · Housing · Central government · Equity

Lastmile

Connectivity at new homes

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A family buys a new-build and discovers there is no gigabit connection and none planned. Building regulations have required every new home to be built connectable since 2022, and nobody checks.

What Lastmile does

Lastmile matches new homes to house-by-house broadband records, producing the compliance picture that has never existed for that rule.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

Public subsidy is targeted at premises that new development has already made commercially viable, because the intervention list and the development pipeline are never compared.

What Lastmile does

Lastmile shows the overlap, so public money goes where companies genuinely will not build on their own.

10 · Climate & energy · Operations · Local government

Bulwark

Flood defence ownership and condition

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A flood defence fails. The council needs to know who was responsible for maintaining it. The national record says "Unknown" — as it does for 73.7% of 141,629 assets.

What Bulwark does

Bulwark works out which piece of land each defence sits on, then who owns that land — and separates genuinely unowned defences from ones where nobody simply wrote it down.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

Maintenance is prioritised using a national condition figure quoted as though it covered the estate. It is computed on the quarter of assets that have a condition grade at all.

What Bulwark does

Bulwark states how much of the country each figure actually covers, and flags defences whose next inspection date has already gone past.

11 · Fraud & integrity · Public money · Operations

Watchman

Insolvency exposure across public suppliers

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A supplier enters administration on a Friday afternoon. Which departments, councils and services depend on it? Nobody can say before Monday, and possibly not for weeks.

What Watchman does

Watchman reads the notice, which includes the company number, and immediately lists everything the public sector relies on that company for. No guesswork about which company it is.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A small supplier files accounts late two years running and takes on new charges over its assets. Both are published signals of difficulty. Nobody is watching, because the standard financial models need profit data small companies never file.

What Watchman does

Watchman treats those as facts that have happened rather than a prediction, and warns the buyers who depend on that supplier.

12 · Local government · Children & families · Public money

Compass

SEND demand forecasting and transport optimisation

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

A county forecasts falling pupil numbers and closes a specialist unit. Two years later demand in one district has risen sharply and children are placed forty miles away at roughly £65,000 a place.

What Compass does

Compass forecasts neighbourhood by neighbourhood instead of county-wide, so the rising district shows up even while the county falls. The National Audit Office found this mismatch in two-thirds of councils.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

A council must submit a SEND reform plan to unlock its share of a £5bn deficit write-off. It cannot evidence where demand will sit in three years, so the plan is assertion rather than analysis.

What Compass does

Compass produces the forecast the plan needs — how many children, needing what, and where — using only published figures. No individual children’s records are touched.

13 · Climate & energy · Public money · Operations

Baseline

Sewage spills, adjusted for the weather

SITUATION 1
What goes wrong today

Spills fell 35% in a year the Environment Agency itself describes as drier than average. A regulator being stood up now must decide whether £22.1bn of investment is working, and has no weather-adjusted figure to judge it by.

What Baseline does

Baseline compares spills against how much rain actually fell nearby, using 1,044 free rain gauges — separating what the money achieved from what the weather did.

SITUATION 2
What goes wrong today

An overflow’s recorded spill count halves — because its monitor was offline for four months. The improvement is an artefact, and the published figure does not say so.

What Baseline does

Baseline shows how long each monitor was actually working next to every spill count, so an unwatched overflow is never mistaken for a quiet one.

The pattern

One problem, thirteen times

Read the twenty-six examples on the system pages and the same sentence turns up in all of them: the record exists, but it cannot be matched to anything else.

The Environment Agency knows it objected to an application. It does not know where. A ministry knows a developer owes £1.49bn. It cannot say for which sites. A council knows a company runs its care homes. It cannot see the other eleven councils that depend on the same company. A regulator knows spills fell 35%. It cannot say how much of that was rain.

None of these is a missing dataset. Every one is a missing link — and there are only two kinds. Work out where. Work out who.

That is why this is one platform and not thirteen separate products. The flood work, the contracts work and the care work are each fairly simple once the data lines up. Getting it to line up is the hard bit — and it is the same hard bit every time.

Fix those two links once, give them away, and thirteen questions nobody can currently answer become answerable. After that it snowballs: one piece of news reaches several systems at once, and every new system costs less than the one before it.
